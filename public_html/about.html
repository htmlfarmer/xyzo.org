Our Mission

XYZO is building the most comprehensive, real-time map of global news ever created. We ingest, analyze, and geolocate news from thousands of sources across every continent, then plot them on an interactive map so you can explore what is happening anywhere on Earth.

But we're not just mapping news. We're mapping life itself — the food festivals and family celebrations, the scientific discoveries and spiritual moments, the community projects and random acts of kindness. Every story matters. Every place matters. Every person matters.

Our goal is to help people understand global news through place and geography, and to reveal the connections between stories that are invisible in standard linear feeds. When you see how a food festival in Tokyo connects to farming in Kenya connects to weather patterns connects to climate connects to trade connects to families — you begin to see the web of life.

We are committed to 100% free and open information — no paywalls, no subscription gates. We strictly respect publisher robots.txt files and terms of service.

How It Works

XYZO watches 2,970+ news sources across 220 countries & territories, ingesting 50,000+ articles daily. Our AI pipeline:

1. Ingests news from RSS feeds, community submissions, and web scraping
2. Extracts locations, topics, and entities using Gemma-4 AI
3. Geocodes stories to exact coordinates using Nominatim
4. Clusters related articles into events (multiple sources = one pin)
5. Connects stories through shared locations, sources, and topics
6. Ranks everything with neural networks that learned from real behavior

The result: a living, breathing map of Earth that shows not just what is happening, but how everything connects.

Why — the story of one of the three maps

There are three maps, and this is the story of the one you are standing on.

The founder's account, in his own words:

"I sat down last night… basically in total pain… and started yelling at my brother's girlfriend or wife… and I was like… even the squirrels in University… are educated… they went this way, got 'here' because… of such an elaborate game?"

He is not a mystic who forgot the physics. He says it himself — "I always respect math and science" — and the thing that broke the argument open was a map.

Standing at Grand Prismatic Spring in Yellowstone, he looked down at the mineral rings — the concentric colour bands built by water and heat, over millions of quiet years — and read them as what they resemble: Antarctica at the centre, the north pole at the rim, a small green patch where Greenland would be. The map was in the ground. It was right there, under his feet, and it was not printed on anything:

"When I 'did see' the 'map of earth' I almost fell to the ground… it was so 'obvious' that I almost died… don't you think it's a little strange that we are left on this planet earth… without any real map of 'the future?'"

Two things came out of that trip, and both of them are what this site is.

For anyone who wants to check the coordinates rather than take anyone's word for it: the GPS and satellite view of Grand Prismatic Spring (44.5250489° N, 110.83819° W), and the technical details of the geyser basins it sits in.

1. A map that can change

The spring is not a picture of a map. It is a map being made, continuously, by water and heat and time — and it is different tomorrow. That is the whole idea of this project. A map of the world that is not a drawing of the world but the world, right now, changing. Every pin on this map is a thing that happened today, at a coordinate, because someone was there and said so.

Which is also why the photographs are not published as stills: the thing they show changes with the water, so a fixed image would be a lie within a day. The live version is the honest one. (The same is true here — and now it is literally true: XYZO keeps a year of the world's weather maps, so the map of the past is a lookup rather than a memory. The ⚙ menu's TIME MACHINE will draw the wind, the temperature and the pressure chart of any day you ask for.)

2. Be careful where you bring electronics

The other thing that trip left him with is a piece of manners, and it is the reason this site's crawler rules are written the way they are:

"We really need to learn to be more wise about where we bring electronics and what we take photos of… I personally feel that no geyser should be allowed to take a photo of… however the smart solution is to 'feel' the ground."

A geyser does not consent to being photographed, and a publisher does not consent to being crawled. XYZO is a machine that reads the world, and it is held to the same courtesy the man with the camera set himself: read what is open to you, go slowly, and do not take more than you need. That is why this site obeys every robots.txt, crawls slowly, and is free of paywalls and registration walls.

The three maps, and which one this is

He is not sure where the other two are — "maybe Australia or Iceland? We really have no idea." Here is what can honestly be said about them:

🦶

The ground

Where you are standing, this instant. You are on one of these maps whether or not you can see it — and it is the one that changes fastest.

🌍

The globe

The whole surface at once, in three dimensions. This site has one: press the 🌐 button on the zoom bar.

🕸️

The web

Not places but connections — the story tree, the nerve system. Click any pin and follow the same story across forty outlets and six continents.

And the one this site is actually about is the third: the map of what people are saying about the world today — assembled honestly, from thousands of publishers, at the coordinates where each thing happened, with no paywall between you and it.

"So… what's the truth? Do we 'now have' THE MAP?"

Not the map of the universe — nobody has that, and anyone who says they do is selling something.

But the map of today: every story, every place, every connection, free and open and updating while you watch. That one exists. It is this page. You are looking at it.

A film is being made about this too — not about the map, but about Idaho, and about what it is to be here. If you want to use this site in it, or want to help make more of the map, the door is open: write to the founder.
